Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Population variability shifts biological mechanics from mode labels to invariant subspaces,”** for publication in *Nature Communications*.

This study addresses a general problem in biological mechanics: how mechanically meaningful deformation pathways should be interpreted across anatomically variable populations. We show that ranked pathways retain stable meaning only while neighbouring modes remain spectrally well separated. Once recurrent clustered regimes emerge, biological function can remain robust at the invariant-subspace level even when rank identity does not.

Using **N = 278** registered human pelvic finite-element models with subject-specific shape and CT-derived bone density, we identify a **gap-protected low-rank backbone** and a set of **recurrent higher-rank clustered subspaces**. Modes 1–3 are rarely reordered across subjects and dominate standing load transfer, whereas higher-rank regions—most prominently the **9–10 pair**—repeatedly enter near-degenerate regimes with label exchange. The 9–10 block provides the clearest proof case because it is prevalent across the cohort, anatomically interpretable through inlet-related coupling, directly label-unstable, and substantially recruited under comparative proxy loading. In particular, AP-inlet coupling shifts between rank labels 9 and 10 when those labels swap, yet remains substantial within the shared 9–10 subspace.

We believe the manuscript is well suited to *Nature Communications* because its main contribution is conceptual rather than system-specific. The human pelvis is used here as a biologically important demonstration system, not as the endpoint of the claim. The broader principle is that biological mechanics under population variability can be partitioned into **indispensable low-rank functions that remain locked to gap-protected pathways** and **higher-rank functions that remain robust through clustered subspaces even when rank labels do not**. This framework should be relevant beyond pelvic biomechanics wherever population variability approaches the scale of spectral separation.

The manuscript is also intentionally scoped. The childbirth-motivated load cases are used only as comparative probes of load recruitment and routing, not as a nonlinear labour simulation or a clinical prediction model. This allows the study to isolate how mechanically available pathways are organized across a population before a specific task is imposed.
