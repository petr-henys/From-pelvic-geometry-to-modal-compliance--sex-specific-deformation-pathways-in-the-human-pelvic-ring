# Draft editorial pitch

**Manuscript:** Pelvic architecture shapes load-dependent sacroiliac motion

Dear Editor,

The pelvis transmits substantial loads through joints that move only slightly. Our study asks how these small movements depend on the architecture of the pelvic ring, and whether apparent sex differences remain when that architecture is described explicitly.

Using imaging-derived anatomy from 278 pelves, we combine five loading configurations with matched geometry–bone-density variants. The central finding is that sacroiliac mobility depends strongly on how a load is distributed: redistributing the same 800 N acetabular force from bilateral to unilateral support increased median bilateral translation asymmetry from 0.065 to 1.602 mm. Localized loads at different anatomical sites produced distinct component responses. Positive age-adjusted female translation differences attenuated after including pelvic dimensions, while retaining individual geometry with a reference bone-density field preserved full-model translation with RMSE no greater than 0.028 mm across loads.

The contribution is the connection between load paths, anatomical variation and joint response. Extending previous population work on SIJ morphology, the paired design tests the same anatomies under different loads and separates individual geometry from bone-density variation under fixed soft-tissue assumptions. This provides an anatomical framework for interpreting pelvic mobility and specific contrasts for experimental validation. The manuscript addresses passive static mechanics; it does not claim to predict delivery outcomes or establish clinical thresholds.

We believe this link between anatomical diversity and mechanical function is relevant to the readership of Journal of Anatomy.

Sincerely,
The authors
