\section{Discussion}
This study used a large, precomputed FE repository to characterize SIJ micromotion under standardized standing and parturition-motivated loading with explicit anatomy and global size-proxy covariates derived from an implicit geometry mapping. The analysis was structured around four pre-specified hypotheses (H1--H4) concerning load dependence and directional re-routing, sex effects under parturition-motivated loading, variance channels, and size scaling. Two general points frame the interpretation. First, the model addresses standardized static loading, so the present results speak most directly to comparative load routing across subjects and load cases rather than to the full time-varying physiology of gait or labor. Second, the strongest geometry result concerns \emph{between-subject dispersion}: geometry dominates how much subjects differ from one another under a fixed loading protocol, whereas the largest shifts in scalar micromotion across the paper are still driven by load case itself.

\subsection*{Interpretation by hypothesis}
\paragraph{H1 (load dependence and re-routing).}
SIJ response was strongly load dependent and direction specific. One-leg standing generated the largest overall SIJ micromotion, consistent with unilateral support demands and with the broader literature showing that routine activities can impose markedly different hip and pelvic loading states \citep{Bergmann2001JBiomech,Kutzner2016PLOS}. In contrast, the parturition-motivated load proxies produced lower global translation magnitude than standing but clear directional re-routing: LAB1 shifted motion toward the ML component, and LAB2 toward both ML and AP components, while both markedly reduced CC translation; LAB3 preserved CC reduction with a distinct ML pattern. These observations support an anisotropic interpretation of SIJ mechanics in which load direction changes the partitioning of motion across AP/ML/CC channels rather than scaling micromotion uniformly \citep{Vleeming2012,Goode2008,Meijer2014,Keizer2019,Noe2020}.

The absolute magnitudes predicted here also remain compatible with the broader picture of the SIJ as a low-motion but load-sensitive articulation. Reviews of SIJ kinematics emphasize both small overall motion and substantial dependence on task and measurement approach, while functional-anatomy work highlights that load distribution and joint position influence the direction and amount of allowed motion \citep{Goode2008,Vleeming2012,Meijer2014}. We therefore interpret the present FE outputs less as direct surrogates for any single \emph{in vivo} experiment and more as a mechanically standardized map of how different loading patterns redistribute motion within the pelvic ring.

\paragraph{H2 (sex effects conditional on anatomy).}
Under parturition-motivated loading, female sex was associated with higher adjusted SIJ micromotion, most consistently for translation magnitude across LAB phases and for rotation magnitude in the LAB3 proxy. Because these effects persisted after adjustment for age, total pelvic volume, and three interpretable pelvic dimensions, they are unlikely to be explained solely by overall body size. Instead, they are consistent with sex-dimorphic pelvic architecture shaping compliance pathways and load routing through the pelvic ring \citep{Ruff2017,Weiss2020}. However, this remains a \emph{coarse} anatomical adjustment, not an exhaustive conditioning on shape: the models do not explicitly encode joint surface curvature, sacral auricular orientation, or ligament attachment geometry. The sex coefficient should therefore be interpreted as a residual group association after limited anatomical conditioning, not as evidence for a single latent sex-specific mechanism.

\paragraph{H3 (geometry-dominant variability).}
Between-subject variability in SIJ outcomes was geometry dominant. Shape-only outputs reproduced essentially all full-model variance, whereas material-only variance fractions were very small. Values slightly above 100\% for the shape/full ratio are not paradoxical: they reflect ratios of sample variances computed on separate simulation variants, not a literal super-additive variance source. Under the present force-standardized loading protocol, these findings indicate that subject-specific morphology and geometry carry most of the inter-subject dispersion signal, while material-only perturbation contributes comparatively little to spread in the tested outcomes.

This result should be read carefully. It does \emph{not} mean that material properties are irrelevant to SIJ mechanics in general; rather, within this repository and for these outputs, geometry explains far more of why subjects differ from one another than the isolated material-only perturbation does. That interpretation is compatible with prior FE work showing sensitivity of pelvic response to ligament restraint, cartilage properties, and loading configuration \citep{Anderson2020,Yang2020,Song2021,Calixte2022}. Our analysis adds that, once loading is standardized across a large cohort, the dominant source of inter-subject spread resides in mapped anatomy.

\paragraph{H4 (size scaling).}
Size scaling was load dependent, but the evidence was more specific than a blanket ``larger pelvis, less motion'' statement. The clearest negative exponents occurred under one-leg standing, and pooled translation models also showed a negative volume association. By contrast, bilateral-standing exponents were imprecise and several LAB-specific exponents crossed zero. Thus, the data support a conditional interpretation: negative scaling is most evident when the loading pattern creates larger unilateral demand or when translation is pooled across load cases, not uniformly across every condition.

This pattern is mechanically plausible in a force-standardized setting, where larger load-bearing geometry may distribute identical resultant loads through broader structural pathways. At the same time, the weak sex-by-size interactions indicate that the dataset does not support strongly different scaling laws for women and men once volume and the coarse morphometric covariates are included. For the current manuscript, the safest conclusion is therefore that allometry modulates SIJ micromotion, but its strength and direction are load dependent.

\subsection*{Anatomical and functional implications}
The combined findings support an anatomy-first interpretation of SIJ mechanics, but with an important distinction between \emph{routing} and \emph{magnitude}. Load direction governs how motion is partitioned across AP/ML/CC channels, whereas mapped pelvic geometry governs most of the between-subject spread under a fixed load definition. From an obstetric-mechanical perspective, this suggests that evaluating only scalar mobility may miss clinically relevant directional behavior, particularly under parturition-motivated loading states.

The observed sex differences in adjusted micromotion, together with established sex-related contrasts in pelvic morphology \citep{Ruff2017,Betti2020,Weber2019,Whitcome2017,Weiss2020}, support the view that group-level differences emerge from load-case-specific routing through a sexually dimorphic pelvic ring rather than from a uniform ``more mobile'' versus ``less mobile'' SIJ phenotype. In other words, the data favor a routing-based interpretation of sex dimorphism: women and men differ not only in how much motion is produced under some loads, but also in how that motion is distributed across anatomical directions when the external load vector is changed.

\subsection*{Limitations}
The analysis remains constrained by the assumptions of the underlying repository. The mechanical model is linear, small-strain, and static; it does not include time-varying contractions, contact-rich fetal-head interactions, or hormonally mediated tissue adaptation \citep{ChenGrimm2021,Almeida2024Uterus,AJOG2022Uterine,BiomechPregnancy2022}. The LAB load cases are therefore proxies, not full labor reconstructions. Material mapping follows a fixed density--modulus law and fixed cartilage/symphysis parameters. In addition, although age was adjusted statistically, the model does not explicitly encode age-dependent tissue remodeling trajectories \citep{FarrKhosla2015,Zebaze2010Porosity}.

Two further limitations are specific to the present statistical framing. First, the anatomical adjustment set is intentionally parsimonious, so residual sex effects cannot be interpreted as fully anatomy-independent. Second, the variance-channel analysis compares separate simulation repositories; it cleanly addresses relative spread under standardized protocols, but it is not a formal variance decomposition of a single stochastic generative model. These boundaries should temper causal interpretation.

\subsection*{Outlook}
Future extensions should combine this population-scale geometry framework with nonlinear contact models and subject-specific soft-tissue updates across pregnancy. A second priority is external validation against \emph{in vivo} SIJ kinematic datasets and imaging-derived pelvic-ring deformation measurements in matched loading scenarios. More generally, the present workflow suggests a useful division of labor for future studies: use large standardized FE cohorts to identify robust routing signatures and variance channels, then test those signatures experimentally in smaller, higher-fidelity cohorts.
