\section{Discussion}
Pelvic mobility emerges here as a response to both architecture and loading. Redistributing the same total acetabular force from two sides to one increased the median bilateral translation asymmetry from 0.065 to 1.602~mm. Localized force pairs elicited distinct component patterns, showing that their effects cannot be summarized as a uniform increase in mobility. Across these configurations, larger pelves showed smaller motions, sex-associated translation differences attenuated after adjustment for pelvic dimensions, and individual geometry preserved much of the response when bone density was standardized. These findings connect population variation in anatomy to the way the pelvic ring responds to different mechanical demands.

\subsection*{Load distribution selects the joint response}
The bilateral--unilateral comparison isolates a simple but consequential change: an 800~N total force is redistributed without increasing its magnitude. Median translation increased from 1.058 to 1.627~mm and median rotation from 0.956 to 2.040$^\circ$, while asymmetry increased much more strongly. The ratio of cohort median asymmetries was approximately 25; this is a comparison of group summaries, not a typical individual fold change. Thus total force alone is a poor description of the mechanical challenge to the SIJs. Its distribution determines moments and load paths through the ring.

The localized probes further show why the direction and location of loading must be considered together. Both transverse force pairs reduced the craniocaudal component relative to bilateral standing, but only the ischial pair increased the mediolateral component after multiplicity correction. The anteroposterior pair produced another combination of component changes. The complete transverse-redistribution hypothesis was therefore not supported: two force pairs acting along the same global axis did not produce interchangeable responses. This result makes application site a substantive anatomical variable, rather than a detail of the loading protocol.

These comparisons complement established accounts of SIJ load transfer \citep{Snijders1993,Vleeming2012}. They identify which loads accentuate asymmetry and which separate motion components, providing specific configurations for subsequent experimental testing. Because the reported components are absolute values in a pelvis-aligned frame, they describe the distribution of motion rather than signed nutation or local joint opening. Obstetric relevance lies in the choice of anatomical probes; birth-canal expansion itself was not measured.

\subsection*{Anatomical dimensions sharpen the interpretation of sex differences}
The sex comparison becomes more informative when considered together with pelvic dimensions. Age-adjusted female translation coefficients were positive under all three localized loads, ranging from 0.055 to 0.258~mm. After including volume, anteroposterior diameter, biischiadic width and subpubic angle, the coefficients ranged from $-0.031$ to $+0.013$~mm, with all intervals spanning zero. The data therefore support a substantial attenuation of the initial group differences when measured anatomy is described explicitly. They do not establish mechanical equivalence between sexes or causal mediation by these dimensions.

The subpubic-angle comparison illustrates why this distinction matters. Its positive pooled association with LAB1 translation ($r_s=0.444$) was absent within either sex. A population association can consequently suggest a simple shape--motion rule that does not hold within the constituent groups. Rather than treating sex as a sufficient mechanical descriptor, the findings motivate analyses of specific dimensions and their combinations. Prediction in new individuals will require external testing, including assessment of the range over which male and female anatomies overlap.

\subsection*{Individual geometry preserves responses under standardized bone density}
The matched variants offer a second route to understanding anatomical variation. With each individual's geometry retained and bone density replaced by a spatially varying population-median field, translation RMSE remained at or below 0.028~mm across loads; rotation RMSE remained at or below 0.032$^\circ$. Minimum identity-line $R^2$ values were 0.946 for translation and 0.878 for rotation. These paired comparisons show that the reference density field preserved individual responses reasonably closely for the reported endpoints, with better agreement for translation than rotation.

The population comparison is consistent with this result: shape-only/full variance ratios had a median of 100.68\%, whereas the corresponding material-only/full median was 0.244\%. Ratios above 100\% are possible because the variants are separate mechanical systems, not terms in an additive variance decomposition. The practical implication is that retaining individual geometry may be a useful priority when constructing models for these joint-motion outputs. Whether density standardization is acceptable in a particular application depends on the error tolerance and endpoint.

This conclusion concerns bone-density variation under the implemented constitutive law. The low-density modulus floor reduces sensitivity to part of the density range, and cartilage and ligament properties were held fixed. Ligament pretension can influence SIJ predictions \citep{Heyland2025}; its population variability could alter the relative importance of anatomy and tissue properties. The present comparison therefore motivates targeted simplification and sensitivity studies, rather than a general claim that material properties are unimportant.

\subsection*{Size contributes consistently across distinct load paths}
All 20 sex-specific volume slopes were negative after multiplicity correction. Their consistency across five loads contrasts with the load-specific component patterns: pelvic size was associated with the magnitude of motion even as the distribution of motion changed. This provides a common structural feature across otherwise different mechanical responses.

A dimensional benchmark helps interpret that consistency. For geometrically similar linear-elastic solids under fixed force and modulus, $u\propto L^{-1}$ and $\theta\sim u/L\propto L^{-2}$, giving volume exponents of $-1/3$ and $-2/3$. The present jointed, heterogeneous pelves also have fixed spring parameters and varying proportions, so these benchmarks are not exact predictions. Moreover, the fitted volume coefficients condition on other dimensions and describe residual size associations. Their range, $-1.062$ to $-0.366$, should consequently be read as load- and endpoint-specific behavior under prescribed force, rather than a universal biological scaling law. Sex-by-volume interactions were not detected; equality of slopes was not tested.

\subsection*{Contribution and next experimental steps}
The contribution beyond previous population work on SIJ morphology \citep{HenysHammer2025} is the connection between paired load responses, whole-pelvis dimensions and matched geometry--density comparisons. The same anatomies are tested under different loads, and the same loads are tested after changing the source of individual variation. Together, these comparisons reveal why a single mobility value or an unadjusted sex contrast misses important features of pelvic mechanics.

The findings suggest focused next experiments: compare bilateral and unilateral support at matched total force, test transverse loads at distinct application sites, and assess whether density standardization preserves measured joint motion. They also motivate direct measurements of signed surface separation and canal dimensions when studying obstetric accommodation. Such measurements would connect the component responses reported here to the functional quantities needed for childbirth models \citep{ChenGrimm2021}.

\subsection*{Scope and limitations}
These are predictions of passive, static mechanics. The fixed sacrum, nominal cartilage properties, linearized ligament representation and absence of muscle activation and frictional contact constrain physiological interpretation. The cohort spans ages 16--91 and does not represent laboring women specifically; pregnancy-related adaptation, parity, pain and delivery outcomes were not analyzed. The results establish neither clinical thresholds nor an evolutionary trade-off.

Motion was measured relative to each subject's unloaded anatomy, keeping shape variation separate from elastic displacement. Affine sacral correction also removes sacral strain; replacing it with rigid-only correction changed translation by at most 0.0055~mm and rotation by 0.0229$^\circ$ in the six-subject sensitivity subset. This is an internal check, not a cohort-wide error bound. Sampling intervals likewise do not include uncertainty in constitutive parameters, mapping or boundary conditions. Quantitative physiological use requires mesh and mapping-quality assessment, constitutive sensitivity and independent experimental validation with matched loads and endpoint definitions, especially given the difficulty of measuring small SIJ motions \citep{Goode2008}.
