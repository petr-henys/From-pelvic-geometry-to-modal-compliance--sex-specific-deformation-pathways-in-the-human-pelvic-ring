\section{Conclusion}
In a 278-subject pelvic FE cohort represented on a common template via an implicit geometry mapping, SIJ micromotion under standardized loads showed three robust features: strong load dependence, clear directional re-routing under parturition-motivated proxies, and geometry-dominant between-subject variability. Female-associated increases in LAB-phase micromotion persisted after adjustment for age, total pelvic volume, and three interpretable pelvic dimensions, but these residual associations should be read as surviving a coarse rather than exhaustive anatomical adjustment. Size scaling was load dependent, with the clearest negative associations under one-leg standing and in pooled translation models, and without robust evidence for sex-divergent scaling slopes. Together, these results support an anatomy-centered view of SIJ mechanics in which load direction governs motion partitioning and pelvic geometry governs most of the inter-subject spread under a standardized loading protocol.
