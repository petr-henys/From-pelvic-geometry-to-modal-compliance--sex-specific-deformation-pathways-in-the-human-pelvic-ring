\section{Conclusion}
Pelvic architecture and load distribution jointly shape sacroiliac motion. At unchanged total force, unilateral support markedly amplified bilateral asymmetry; localized loads selected distinct motion-component patterns. Sex-associated translation differences attenuated when pelvic dimensions were included, while larger pelves showed smaller responses across all five loads. Preserving individual geometry with a reference bone-density field retained much of the individual response and population variation. These controlled comparisons provide an anatomical basis for interpreting pelvic mobility and concrete targets for experiments that connect joint motion to pelvic function.
