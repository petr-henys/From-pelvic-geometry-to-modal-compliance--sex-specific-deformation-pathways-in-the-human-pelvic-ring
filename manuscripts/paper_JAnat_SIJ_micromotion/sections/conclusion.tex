\section{Conclusion}
Under standardized loading, pelvic size showed a consistent inverse conditional association with SIJ motion, while individual geometry retained much of the subject-specific response under standardized bone density and fixed soft-tissue properties. Load distribution and application site determined how that response was expressed. Explicit pelvic dimensions also substantially attenuated sex-associated differences. Together, these comparisons support describing pelvic mechanics through anatomical scale, individual architecture and loading context rather than a single sex-specific mobility value. They do not establish causality, sex equivalence, universal biological scaling or physiological validation.
