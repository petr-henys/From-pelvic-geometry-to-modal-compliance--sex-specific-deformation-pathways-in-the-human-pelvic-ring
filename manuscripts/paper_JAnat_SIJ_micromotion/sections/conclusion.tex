\section{Conclusion}
In 278 pelvic finite-element models, unilateral acetabular loading increased joint motion and bilateral translation asymmetry relative to bilateral loading. Localized force pairs produced distinct component responses, rather than a shared pattern of transverse opening. No fully adjusted female translation association survived multiplicity correction, while conditional volume slopes were negative across all five loads. Shape-only models broadly retained full-model variability, with endpoint-dependent individual errors. These findings support reproducible comparisons of specified passive mechanical configurations. Claims about obstetric adaptation, force closure, pain or surgical benefit require measurements not provided by these simulations.
