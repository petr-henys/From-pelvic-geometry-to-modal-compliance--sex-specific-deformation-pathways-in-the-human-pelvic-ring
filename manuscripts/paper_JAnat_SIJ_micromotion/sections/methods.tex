\section{Materials and methods}
\subsection{Cohort characteristics and ethical governance}
We investigated a standardized finite-element pelvic repository and two matched auxiliary variant datasets developed to isolate structural shape from bone tissue material heterogeneity \citep{HenyssKuchar2025BoneDat}. The cohort comprised 278 complete human pelvic specimens (150 female, 128 male) spanning an adult lifespan of 16 to 91 years (mean $\pm$ SD: 53.9 $\pm$ 19.3 years). All models were generated from high-resolution clinical computed tomography (CT) scans curated within the open BoneDat anatomical repository \citep{HenyssKuchar2025BoneDat}. Use of these anonymized imaging data was approved by the Ethics Committee of the University Hospital Hradec Kr\'alov\'e (reference 202411IO3P) with a waiver of informed consent.

Eight standardized linear and angular morphometric landmarks were extracted for each pelvis (Table~\ref{tab:cohort}): anteroposterior (AP) inlet diameter, biacetabular width, biischiadic width, bituberous width, iliopectineal eminence width, pelvic inlet transverse (PIT) diameter, sacral width, and subpubic arch angle (see Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}A and Appendix~\ref{app:mathematical_formulation} for landmark definitions of the primary morphometric triad). Morphometric completeness, demographic metadata, global volume metrics, and SIJ kinematic endpoints exhibited zero missing values across the 278 subjects.

\subsection{Finite-element modeling and geometry parameterization}
Simulations were formulated under a 3D small-strain continuum elastostatic framework posed on a shared, high-fidelity reference pelvic template \citep{BonetWood1997,MarsdenHughes1983} and solved using FEniCSx/DOLFINx (v0.10) with PETSc linear algebraic solvers. Subject-specific anatomy was parameterized through a smooth, invertible displacement vector field $\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbf{X})$, mapping reference spatial coordinates $\mathbf{X}$ to individual anatomy $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{X} + \boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbf{X})$ via radial basis function interpolation \citep{HenyssKuchar2025BoneDat}. This implicit mapping avoids mesh discretization discrepancies across subjects while preserving exact individual curvature, cortical boundaries, and joint orientations. Conforming, continuous meshes were maintained across bone--cartilage interfaces at both sacroiliac joints and the pubic symphysis.

Bony material properties were assigned by mapping voxel-level CT attenuation values into apparent bone mineral density and computing local elastic moduli via established power-law formulations \citep{CarterHayes1977,Keller1994,Morgan2003Trabecular,Frost1987,Huiskes1987}:
\begin{equation}
E = \begin{cases}
2398\;\text{MPa}, & \rho_{\text{ash}} \le 0.486\\
10200\,\rho_{\text{ash}}^{2}\;\text{MPa}, & \rho_{\text{ash}} > 0.486
\end{cases},
\quad
\rho_{\text{ash}} = \frac{\rho + 0.09}{1.14},
\end{equation}
with an isotropic Poisson's ratio $\nu = 0.30$. The bilateral sacroiliac joint cartilages and the fibrocartilaginous pubic symphysis were modeled with nominal linear-elastic properties ($E = 1.0\;\text{MPa}, \nu = 0.45$), consistent with established pelvic finite-element parameterizations \citep{Anderson2020,Yang2020,Calixte2022}. The multi-ligamentous pelvic apparatus was represented explicitly via discrete spring elements with non-linear tensile stiffness and nominal pretension, incorporating anterior sacroiliac, posterior/dorsal sacroiliac, interosseous sacroiliac, sacrotuberous, sacrospinous, and pubic ligament complexes \citep{Hammer2013,Hammer2019}.

\subsection{Standardized physiological loading regimes}
In all configurations, the pelvic ring was constrained at the superior S1 sacral facet endplate via a Dirichlet boundary penalty condition ($\gamma = 1.0 \times 10^6$). Five standardized static load configurations were evaluated (Table~\ref{tab:loads}; Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}B--D):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Two-Leg Stance (SP2leg):} Symmetrical bipedal stance under bilateral vertical upward ground reaction forces of $+400$~N applied to each acetabular notch (resultant $+800$~N craniocaudal force reacting against the fixed sacrum).
\item \textbf{Single-Leg Stance (SP1leg):} Unilateral stance phase under a $+800$~N vertical upward ground reaction force applied to the right acetabular notch (resultant $+800$~N craniocaudal force), simulating single-limb support with an unsupported contralateral hemi-pelvis.
\item \textbf{Labor Phase 1 (LAB1 - Internal Ring Compression/Shear Proxy):} Mediolateral force pair ($\pm 400$~N; $+400$~N on left, $-400$~N on right internal pelvic ring contact surfaces), modeling inward/transverse head engagement during early fetal descent.
\item \textbf{Labor Phase 2 (LAB2 - Ischial Distraction Proxy):} Bilateral mediolateral distraction under $\pm 400$~N outward forces applied to the left and right ischial tuberosities ($+400$~N left, $-400$~N right), representing midplane transverse dilation.
\item \textbf{Labor Phase 3 (LAB3 - Outlet AP Distraction Proxy):} Anteroposterior outlet distraction under $\pm 400$~N opposing forces acting between the anterior pubic insertion ($-400$~N anteriorly) and the posterior sacrococcygeal complex ($+400$~N posteriorly), simulating sagittal outlet expansion during fetal expulsion.
\end{enumerate}
Forces were applied as standardized surface tractions integrated over designated anatomical surface regions, scaled inversely by surface area to ensure identical resultant force magnitudes across all individual pelvic morphologies (see Appendix~\ref{app:mathematical_formulation} for complete mathematical details of the boundary value problem and traction integrals).

\subsection{SIJ kinematics and local joint coordinate triad}
Relative motion of the ilium with respect to the sacrum was extracted from the deformed nodal fields of the bilateral articulation surfaces using trimmed rigid registration (Kabsch algorithm) on the anatomical regions of interest \citep{HenyssKuchar2025BoneDat}. A complete mathematical specification of pelvic coordinate frame construction, affine sacrum drift removal, trimmed Kabsch registration, and Cardan angle extraction is detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:mathematical_formulation}. Kinematics were projected onto an anatomical joint coordinate triad following a Cardan rotation sequence ($XYZ$ order; Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}D):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Nutation ($\theta_{\text{nut}} = \theta_x$):} Sagittal rotation around the joint transverse axis (positive = anterior sacral tilt/nutation, negative = posterior sacral tilt/counternutation).
\item \textbf{Abduction ($\theta_y$):} Frontal tilt around the anteroposterior axis.
\item \textbf{Axial Rotation ($\theta_z$):} Transverse twist around the craniocaudal axis.
\item \textbf{Translations (dx, dy, dz):} 3D translation components along the mediolateral (ML; dx), anteroposterior (AP; dy), and craniocaudal (CC; dz) axes.
\end{itemize}
Scalar kinematic endpoints were defined as the 3D resultant rotation magnitude,
\begin{equation}
|\boldsymbol{\theta}| = \frac{1}{2} \left( \|\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{left}}\| + \|\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{right}}\| \right),
\end{equation}
and 3D resultant translation magnitude,
\begin{equation}
|\mathbf{d}| = \frac{1}{2} \left( \|\mathbf{d}_{\text{left}}\| + \|\mathbf{d}_{\text{right}}\| \right),
\end{equation}
evaluated as the bilateral mean across left and right SIJs. Component motions reported in summary tables and statistical contrasts represent bilateral means of absolute component magnitudes ($0.5 [|t_{L, k}| + |t_{R, k}|]$ for $k \in \{\text{AP}, \text{ML}, \text{CC}\}$ and nutation $| \theta_{x} |$), preventing sign-cancellation across bilateral mirror symmetry. Bilateral asymmetry was quantified as the absolute inter-side difference: $\Delta_{\text{asym}}|\mathbf{d}| = |d_{\text{left}} - d_{\text{right}}|$.

\subsection{Variance-channel decomposition and paired diagnostics}
To decouple macroscopic geometry from tissue material properties, each subject's response was evaluated across three matched simulation repositories:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Full Model:} Subject-specific mapped geometry $\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbf{X})$ and subject-specific bone density mapping $E(\mathbf{x})$.
\item \textbf{Shape-Only Model:} Subject-specific mapped geometry $\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbf{X})$ evaluated with homogeneous, population-median reference bone material properties.
\item \textbf{Material-Only Model:} Subject-specific bone density mapping $E(\mathbf{x})$ applied to the unmapped reference template geometry ($\boldsymbol{\phi} = \mathbf{0}$).
\end{enumerate}
Importantly, "material-only" isolates cortical and trabecular bone mineral density (modulus) heterogeneity, while cartilage and ligament properties remain nominal. For each load case and kinematic metric, variance retention ratios were computed as $V_{\text{shape}} / V_{\text{full}}$ and $V_{\text{material}} / V_{\text{full}}$, with 95\% confidence intervals estimated via 2,000 bootstrap iterations \citep{EfronTibshirani1994}. Because these ratios compare sample variances across separate datasets, they represent variance retention ratios rather than an orthogonal additive decomposition. To assess subject-level agreement between the Full and Shape-Only models, we evaluated paired diagnostics including the coefficient of determination against the 1:1 identity line ($R^2$), Pearson and Spearman correlation coefficients, root-mean-square error (RMSE), and mean absolute error (MAE).

\subsection{Statistical analysis}
Continuous outcomes were summarized as medians and interquartile ranges [IQR]. Directional re-routing was tested by computing subject-wise differences against baseline bilateral stance ($\Delta = \text{Outcome}_{\text{load}} - \text{Outcome}_{\text{SP2leg}}$) and evaluating shifts with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests \citep{Wilcoxon1945}.

To evaluate sex-related differences and morphometric associations under parturition loads (H3), we fitted nested ordinary least squares (OLS) regressions with HC3 heteroskedasticity-consistent standard errors \citep{White1980,MacKinnonWhite1985}:
\begin{align}
\text{Model 0 (M0):}\quad Y &= \beta_0 + \beta_{\text{sex}}\cdot\text{Sex} + \beta_{\text{age}}\cdot\text{Age}_z + \epsilon, \label{eq:m0} \\
\text{Model 1 (M1):}\quad Y &= \beta_0 + \beta_{\text{sex}}\cdot\text{Sex} + \beta_{\text{age}}\cdot\text{Age}_z + \beta_{\text{vol}}\cdot\log(\text{Vol})_z + \epsilon, \label{eq:m1} \\
\text{Model 2 (M2):}\quad Y &= \beta_0 + \beta_{\text{sex}}\cdot\text{Sex} + \beta_{\text{age}}\cdot\text{Age}_z + \beta_{\text{vol}}\cdot\log(\text{Vol})_z \nonumber \\
  &\quad +\; \beta_{\text{AP}}\cdot\text{AP}_z + \beta_{\text{ischiadic}}\cdot\text{Biischiadic}_z + \beta_{\text{subpubic}}\cdot\text{Subpubic}_z + \epsilon, \label{eq:m2}
\end{align}
where continuous predictors were standardized ($z$-scores). The morphometric triad (AP diameter, biischiadic width, and subpubic angle; defined geometrically in Appendix~\ref{app:mathematical_formulation}) was chosen \emph{a priori} to capture inlet depth, transverse outlet span, and anterior arch opening. Multicollinearity among predictors was screened using Variance Inflation Factors (VIF). Progressive attenuation of $\beta_{\text{sex}}$ across M0 $\to$ M1 $\to$ M2 evaluated the proportion of sex-related mobility attributable to size and architectural dimorphism. In addition, bivariate associations between kinematic compliance and individual morphometric landmarks were examined using Spearman rank correlations.

Repeated measures across all five load cases were analyzed using cluster-robust OLS clustered at the subject level. Secondary allometric size-scaling relationships were evaluated using log--log power-law regressions:
\begin{equation}
\log(Y) = \alpha + \beta_{\text{vol}}\cdot\log(\text{Total Volume}) + \beta_{\text{sex}}\cdot\text{Sex} + \beta_{\text{int}}\cdot[\log(\text{Total Volume})\times\text{Sex}] + \mathbf{Z}\boldsymbol{\gamma} + \epsilon,
\end{equation}
extracting sex-stratified allometric scaling exponents ($\beta_{\text{male}}$ and $\beta_{\text{female}}$) with Wald tests for non-zero slopes. Multiplicity across hypothesis tests was controlled using the Benjamini--Hochberg false discovery rate (BH-FDR, $q < 0.05$) \citep{BenjaminiHochberg1995}.
