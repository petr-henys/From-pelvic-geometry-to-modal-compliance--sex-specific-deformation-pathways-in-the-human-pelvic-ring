Sex describes population differences in pelvic form, but its adequacy as a mechanical descriptor depends on how much information explicit anatomy adds. We examined sacroiliac joint responses in 278 imaging-derived pelves using five standardized loading configurations and matched geometry--bone-density variants. Under three localized force pairs, age-adjusted female-minus-male translation coefficients ranged from +0.055 to +0.258~mm. Including pelvic volume, inlet depth, interspinous width and subpubic angle attenuated these coefficients to -0.031 to +0.013~mm, with all fully adjusted confidence intervals spanning zero. This attenuation does not establish sex equivalence or causal mediation. Preserving individual geometry while standardizing the bone-density field retained subject-specific translation, with RMSE no greater than 0.028~mm and minimum identity-line $R^2=0.946$ across loads. Load path nevertheless changed the response: redistributing the same total acetabular force from bilateral to unilateral support amplified asymmetry, and transverse force pairs applied at different sites produced distinct component patterns. Explicit pelvic architecture and loading thus provide a more informative description of modeled sacroiliac response than sex alone. The geometry--density comparison is conditional on the implemented density--modulus law, fixed cartilage and ligament properties and pretension, and passive static mechanics.
