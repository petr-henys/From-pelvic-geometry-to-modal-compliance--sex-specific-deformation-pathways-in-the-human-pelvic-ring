How much of population sacroiliac joint (SIJ) motion can be predicted from size and a few anatomical measurements, and what information is needed when loading changes? We analyzed 278 imaging-derived pelvic models under five standardized loads using passive linear finite-element (FE) mechanics. This cohort is a subset of a previously studied computed tomography collection. Repeated subject-held-out cross-validation compared age-and-volume models with models adding inlet anteroposterior diameter, interspinous width and subpubic angle. The triad reduced translation root mean square prediction error (RMSE) by 8.2--31.3\% across loads, with prediction $R^2=0.309$--0.717. Rotation gains ranged from 1.2\% to 25.0\%; the smallest gain had a pointwise interval including zero. Adding sex terms or all eight available dimensions gave no clear further improvement in paired intervals. A controlled mechanistic test redistributed the same transverse forces between internal-ring and ischial patches, using exact FE field superposition and re-extracting joint motion. At equal redistribution, translation was a median 21.9\% below the scalar combination of individual endpoint magnitudes (95\% confidence interval 20.0--23.7\%). Retaining signed endpoint vectors reduced combination RMSE from 0.0580 to 0.00021~mm. This identifies directional cancellation as a limit to scalar load transfer, separately from cross-subject prediction error. Geometry-preserving density standardization retained individual translation closely (RMSE $\leq0.0280$~mm), conditional on the implemented material law and shared soft-tissue properties. A compact anatomical description therefore predicts useful variation under specified loads, while transfer to redistributed loading requires directional information. Validation remains internal and the predictor set was selected retrospectively.
