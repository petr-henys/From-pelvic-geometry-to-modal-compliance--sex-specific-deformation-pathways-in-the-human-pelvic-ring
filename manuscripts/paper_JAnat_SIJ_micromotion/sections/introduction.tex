\section{Introduction}
The pelvis must transmit substantial loads through joints that move only slightly. This combination makes the sacroiliac joint (SIJ) an informative site at which to study how skeletal architecture organizes movement: small changes in relative motion can reveal how a load travels through the pelvic ring. Articular geometry, ligamentous restraint and muscular compression together contribute to pelvic stability \citep{Vleeming2012,Snijders1993,Hammer2013}. Yet a description of how much the SIJ moves is incomplete without specifying where the load acts and which anatomy carries it.

This problem also changes how pelvic sexual dimorphism should be interpreted mechanically. Female and male pelves differ in dimensions relevant to both load transfer and obstetric anatomy. A difference in joint motion between these groups could reflect overall size, particular dimensions, or anatomical features beyond the measurements available. Distinguishing these possibilities is more informative than assigning each sex a single degree of pelvic mobility. The relationship between locomotor support and obstetric accommodation gives this question biological importance \citep{Pavlicev2020}; mechanical models can address its structural basis, while simulations of childbirth additionally require fetal contact, soft tissues and changing boundary conditions \citep{ChenGrimm2021}.

Previous population modeling has linked SIJ morphology to its mechanical environment \citep{HenysHammer2025}. The next question is how the same pelvis responds when the load path changes, and whether the resulting differences among individuals follow pelvic architecture or bone-density variation. Addressing this experimentally across many anatomies is difficult: loading and tissue properties cannot readily be varied independently in vivo. Matched computational comparisons provide a way to hold these factors constant in turn. Their value lies in the controlled comparisons they enable, alongside the anatomical diversity they retain.

Here, we combine imaging-derived anatomy from 278 pelves with five standardized load configurations and three matched geometry--density variants, using the BoneDat workflow \citep{HenyssKuchar2025BoneDat}. Bilateral and unilateral acetabular loading redistribute the same total force. Three further force pairs probe the ring at sites motivated by obstetric anatomy; they are independent mechanical probes, not successive stages of labor. This design links three scales of explanation: the load applied to the ring, the motion expressed at its joints, and the anatomical differences among individuals.

We organize the analysis around four hypotheses. \textbf{H1:} shifting from bilateral to unilateral support increases SIJ motion and bilateral asymmetry. \textbf{H2:} the two transverse force pairs increase the absolute mediolateral component and reduce the craniocaudal component relative to bilateral support; the anteroposterior pair is assessed separately. \textbf{H3:} greater female translation under the localized force pairs is attenuated when pelvic size and dimensions are included. \textbf{H4:} retaining individual geometry while standardizing the bone-density field largely preserves both between-subject variation and individual joint responses. Volume--motion associations provide a complementary, exploratory test of the role of pelvic size. Together, these comparisons ask whether pelvic mobility is better understood as a response specific to anatomy and loading than as a fixed characteristic of a sex or an individual.
