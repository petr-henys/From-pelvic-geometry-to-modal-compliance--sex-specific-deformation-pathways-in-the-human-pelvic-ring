\section{Introduction}
The sacroiliac joints (SIJs) transmit load between the spine and lower limbs through a pelvic ring stabilized by articular geometry, ligaments and muscular compression \citep{Snijders1993,Vleeming2012,Hammer2013}. Cadaveric experiments with bodyweight-adapted loading measured small, three-dimensional SIJ movements and substantial interindividual variation \citep{Hammer2019}. Joint motion therefore provides a functional endpoint for examining how pelvic anatomy responds to a specified load.

Pelvic variation spans overall size, inlet and midpelvic proportions, pubic arch geometry and local SIJ morphology. Sex differences in obstetrically relevant dimensions develop along distinct age-related trajectories \citep{Huseynov2016}. Pelvic shape also covaries with stature \citep{Fischer2015}. \citet{Fischer2017} distinguished a largely stature-related component of sexual dimorphism in pelvic height-to-width proportions and iliac orientation from predominantly non-allometric differences in the subpubic angle. At the joint itself, analysis of Japanese skeletal specimens identified sex differences in iliac auricular surface size and proportions \citep{Nishi2020}. These findings motivate separate consideration of size, specific dimensions and sex in mechanical comparisons.

The mechanical consequences of pelvic form are relevant to both load transmission and obstetric anatomy \citep{Pavlicev2020}. Finite-element models of the pelvic floor showed increasing deflection and tissue stress with increasing floor size under prescribed pressure \citep{Stansfield2021}. For the bony pelvic ring, the corresponding question concerns the relationship between its dimensions and joint motion under prescribed forces. Anatomical covariation with stature and mechanical response to standardized loading address distinct aspects of size dependence.

Evidence for sex-associated SIJ motion comes from computational, imaging and cadaveric studies. \citet{Joukar2018} found greater mobility in a female than in a male lumbopelvic finite-element model under identical loading. Upright MRI in 19 healthy young adults showed greater sagittal SIJ rotation in women during the change from supine to standing \citep{Tani2023}. A study of 24 cadavers likewise found greater range of motion in females, while measured auricular surface dimensions did not distinguish groups with high and low mobility \citep{Shin2024}. Population models permit assessment of how sex-associated contrasts change after adjustment for pelvic volume and dimensions, and how these anatomical associations vary with loading configuration.

Computational studies also connect anatomy and material representation to SIJ mechanics. Population modeling linked auricular surface area and cartilage volume to local stress, strain and strain energy \citep{HenysHammer2025}. Other models identified sensitivity of SIJ response to ligament pretension \citep{Heyland2025} and increased motion following reductions in bone stiffness used to represent osteoporosis \citep{Fan2026}. Matched geometry--density comparisons address the contribution of the represented bone-density field to individual motion predictions. Retaining subject-specific geometry with a standardized density field, and individual density on a common geometry, permits direct comparison with the full subject-specific model while holding soft-tissue properties fixed.

Here, we examined SIJ response in 278 imaging-derived pelves from the BoneDat workflow \citep{HenyssKuchar2025BoneDat} under five standardized loading configurations. Bilateral and unilateral acetabular loads redistribute a fixed total force; localized force pairs probe different application sites on the ring. These are controlled mechanical probes. Childbirth models additionally represent fetal passage and maternal soft-tissue deformation \citep{ChenGrimm2021}. We evaluated conditional volume--motion associations with other pelvic dimensions held fixed, changes in sex-associated coefficients after anatomical adjustment, and preservation of individual responses under standardized bone density. All subjects received identical prescribed forces within each load case. The analyses were retrospective; the conditional scaling analysis was exploratory.
