\begin{abstract}
The human sacroiliac joint (SIJ) functions at the biomechanical interface between bipedal weight-bearing stability and cephalopelvic obstetric clearance. Under the paradigm of form and force closure, axial locomotor loads engage self-bracing nutation, but how multi-axial parturition-motivated forces re-route joint kinematics, the degree of sex-related mechanical compliance, and the relative contributions of skeletal geometry versus bone tissue material heterogeneity remain unresolved across population cohorts. We evaluated a precomputed finite-element cohort of 278 human pelves (150 female, 128 male; age 16--91 years) parameterized via an implicit subject-specific continuous displacement mapping on a shared reference template. Five standardized static loading regimes were analyzed with the superior S1 facet constrained via a Dirichlet penalty: bilateral standing (SP2leg; $+400$~N vertical ground reaction on each acetabular notch, 800~N total), unilateral standing (SP1leg; $+800$~N on the right acetabulum), and three parturition-motivated multi-axial expansion configurations (LAB1--LAB3; $\pm 400$~N internal ring compression, ischial distraction, and outlet distraction proxies). Kinematics were resolved in a local joint coordinate triad, tracking bilateral means of 3D resultant norms ($|\boldsymbol{\theta}|$, $|\mathbf{d}|$) and absolute component magnitudes. We tested directional contrasts relative to bilateral standing, assessed sex-related differences through nested multivariable linear regressions (M0: sex and age; M1: +pelvic volume; M2: +morphometric triad) with heteroskedasticity-consistent standard errors (HC3) and variance inflation factor screening, evaluated variance retention ratios ($V_{\text{shape}} / V_{\text{full}}$, $V_{\text{material}} / V_{\text{full}}$) with paired subject-level diagnostics, and explored allometric power-law scaling with false discovery rate control ($q < 0.05$). Stance regimes engaged physiological nutation (median $1.06^\circ$ in SP2leg, $1.16^\circ$ in SP1leg). Unilateral stance imposed the highest kinematic demand ($|\boldsymbol{\theta}| = 2.52^\circ$, $|\mathbf{d}| = 1.76$~mm), driving bilateral translation asymmetry from $0.21$~mm in SP2leg to $2.01$~mm. Under parturition proxies, sagittal nutation was suppressed ($0.41^\circ$--$0.43^\circ$ in LAB1/LAB2), and displacement shifted from vertical craniocaudal shear ($\Delta\text{CC} = -0.59$ to $-0.90$~mm) to transverse outward distraction ($\Delta\text{ML} = +0.09$ to $+0.15$~mm). In multivariable models, female pelves exhibited a significant translational surplus across labor regimes ($+0.19$ to $+0.23$~mm, $p \le 0.020$), which attenuated by approximately 38\% upon adjusting for volume and morphometric dimensions; transverse compliance was positively associated with subpubic arch angle ($r = 0.52, p < 10^{-19}$). Variance decomposition demonstrated geometric dominance: shape-only models retained 98.7\%--102.1\% (median 100.5\%) of full-model variance ($R^2 \ge 0.994$, $\text{RMSE} \le 0.031$~mm), whereas bone mineral density heterogeneity accounted for $<0.32\%$ (median 0.033\%). Negative allometric volume scaling was strongest in unilateral stance ($\beta_{\text{male}} = -0.57, \beta_{\text{female}} = -0.43, q \le 0.004$). Under standardized static loading, SIJ micromotion displays marked directional re-routing: locomotor loading reinforces self-bracing nutation, whereas parturition-motivated regimes direct compliance into transverse ring expansion. Between-subject kinematic variation is governed predominantly by pelvic geometry rather than bone mineralization, clarifying the passive mechanical compromise between bipedal stability and pelvic compliance.
\end{abstract}
