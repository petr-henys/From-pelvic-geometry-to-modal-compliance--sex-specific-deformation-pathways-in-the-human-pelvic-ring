\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Background:} Sacroiliac joint (SIJ) micromotion depends on pelvic anatomy, material properties, and load direction, yet large-cohort evidence under standardized standing and parturition-motivated loading remains limited, particularly for separating geometric versus material sources of between-subject variability.

\noindent\textbf{Methods:} We analyzed a precomputed finite-element repository of 278 pelves (150 female, 128 male; age 16--91 years) represented on a common template via an implicit subject-specific mapping. Five standardized static load cases were studied: two-leg standing (SP2leg), one-leg standing (SP1leg), and three parturition-motivated proxies (LAB1--LAB3). Outcomes were SIJ rotation/translation magnitudes and AP/ML/CC translation components. We used robust and cluster-robust regression with adjustment for age, total pelvic volume, and three a priori pelvic dimensions (AP diameter, biischiadic width, subpubic angle), plus variance-channel ratios (shape-only/full and material-only/full) and log--log size-scaling models.

\noindent\textbf{Results:} SP1leg produced the largest SIJ motion (median rotation 2.52$^\circ$; translation 1.76 mm). Relative to SP2leg, LAB loads reduced translation magnitude but showed clear directional re-routing of AP/ML/CC components. After coarse anatomical adjustment, female sex was associated with higher LAB translation magnitude (0.20--0.23 mm across LAB1--LAB3) and higher LAB3 rotation. Between-subject variability was overwhelmingly geometry-dominant (shape/full 98.7--102.1\%), whereas material-only variance fractions were small (0.001--0.317\%). Negative size scaling was clearest for SP1leg and for pooled translation models; sex-by-size slope interactions were not robust after false-discovery correction.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusions:} Under standardized forces, SIJ response is strongly load dependent, and between-subject dispersion is dominated by pelvic geometry rather than material mapping. Parturition-motivated loads alter the directional partitioning of SIJ motion more clearly than they increase its scalar magnitude. Size scaling is load dependent, with the clearest negative associations under one-leg standing and in pooled translation models.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} sacroiliac joint; pelvic anatomy; finite-element analysis; sexual dimorphism; scaling; parturition biomechanics
